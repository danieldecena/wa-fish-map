Inside
WAFishMap

A statewide fishing map for Washington that answers one question — is it worth going here, this month? — and then sends you to WDFW to check the rules.

SwiftUI + MapKit iPhone & iPad ~1,440 lines of Swift 17 marine areas Live WDFW ArcGIS
The premise

Geography from WDFW, timing from a curated file

The app fuses two things that are usually kept apart. The geography is live and official: marine-area polygons, water-access sites, and public piers pulled straight from WDFW's ArcGIS map server at launch. The timing is hand-curated and offline: a single bundled JSON file that says which species are worth chasing in which months.

Cross the two and every pin on the map gets a color for the month you're planning for. Green means something is peaking there; red means nothing much is. Tap a pin and you get the reasoning — species, best months, best light, bait, and a note.

What it does

  • Statewide map of every WDFW launch, shore access, and public pier
  • Marine-area polygons tinted by this month's rating
  • A date picker — set it to October and the whole map recolors
  • A heat overlay showing where well-rated spots cluster
  • Detail sheets with what's biting, and a link out to WDFW

What it deliberately isn't

  • Not the rulebook — Fish Washington stays authoritative
  • No live bag limits, no scraped season or closure dates
  • No public catch heatmap, no crowd-sourced reports
  • The rating is editorial timing, never a claim that a fishery is open
The screen

One map, two floating bars, one sheet

There is exactly one screen. ContentView renders FishingMapView and nothing else — no tab bar, no navigation stack over the map. Everything else floats on top of it as glass cards, so the map is never boxed in.

The rating engine

Four steps from a month to a color

Every species entry in the data carries bestMonths and goodMonths. A species' rating for a given month falls out of set membership, with one wrinkle: months sit on a circle, so December and January are neighbors.

An area's rating is the best any single species reaches there that month — one peaking run is enough to turn a whole polygon green. The sheet's "what's biting" list then shows every species at Fair or better, sorted best-first with declaration order breaking ties so the list doesn't shuffle between renders.

The logic is deliberately pure: it never reads the clock. The view injects the month from the selected date, which is what makes the whole thing unit-testable — and what makes the date picker work at all.

The data path

Three live layers, one bundled file

On launch, FishingMapModel.load() fetches three WDFW ArcGIS layers as GeoJSON, decodes them, and joins them against the bundled guidance. Launches don't carry a marine-area code, so the join is geometric.

The heat overlay

Density of good spots, on a different ramp

Toggling Heat buckets every launch and pier into a 0.15° lon/lat grid, weighting each by its rating — a Prime spot counts 2.0, a Slow one 1.0 — then normalizes to the densest cell and draws one 9 km circle per cell. It answers "where should I even be looking" rather than "is this spot good".

Its ramp runs blue → purple → red, chosen specifically so it can't be confused with the green/yellow/orange/red rating palette underneath it. The legend at the bottom swaps to match whichever mode is on. Zones are cached in view state and recomputed only on toggle, month change, or data load — not on every render pass.

CI runs it on every push and again weekly, so upstream drift surfaces on its own rather than as a blank map.

Open threads

What the project knows it's missing

Closures aren't modeled A month can rate Prime while the fishery is legally shut. The data has been hand-corrected for the worst cases — blackmouth in Areas 10 and 11, lingcod in July — but open-window modeling is a real feature that hasn't started.

Guidance updates only on release Geography self-updates every launch; fisheries.json ships with the binary. That's a deliberate choice, but it means a correction needs an App Store trip.

One polygon is unseeded Fifteen of the sixteen seeded areas plus twelve counties are WDFW-checked; the "Oregon" boundary polygon has no entry and renders neutral.

Fetches are unpaginated By design — the verifier warns at 80% of the record cap, and layer 0 sits at 477 of 2000. Pagination only if that ever fires.
